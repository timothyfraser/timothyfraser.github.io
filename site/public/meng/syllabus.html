📋 M.Eng. Project Syllabus

Six M.Eng. team projects building big-data and AI systems to measure and track outcomes across urban and environmental systems — air quality, congestion pricing, mobility networks, and disaster resilience.

Project overview

Build big-data and AI systems to measure and track outcomes across urban and environmental systems — air quality, congestion pricing, mobility networks, and disaster resilience. Six M.Eng. team projects run this year, each with its own team, its own registration section, and its own product. Each project ships a report plus a working tool.

Project areas Emissions, Transportation, Air Quality, Social Infrastructure, Reliability, Sensors, Data Communication, Visualization, System Architecture, Databases, Dashboards, Algorithms

Enrollment cap 5 students per project

The six projects

SYSEN 5900-648 Class nbr — 🚗 MOVESAI

AI-Powered Emissions Reporting Systems with MOVESAI

Machine LearningDashboardsEmissions

Can AI reproduce a heavyweight EPA emissions model fast enough for any city to use online?

Transportation is a top source of greenhouse gases and air pollution, but the models that estimate it are slow and hard for local agencies to use. You'll build AI and machine-learning systems that reproduce EPA's MOVES emissions simulator and serve it through fast APIs and dashboards — so cities can estimate and report their transportation emissions online without running the full model. Work spans machine learning, API design, front-end dashboards, and the report that ships with it.

Enrollment cap: 5 students Surrogate → Dashboard → Report
SYSEN 5900-662 Class nbr 19053 🏙️ Urban Air

Urban Air Quality and Traffic Data Systems with CIVIC

Air QualityTransportationCausal Inference

When a city charges drivers to enter its core, does the air actually get cleaner — and who benefits?

When a city changes how people move — congestion pricing, new transit, road redesigns — does the air actually get cleaner, and for whom? You'll build the data systems behind CIVIC: integrating air quality, transit, and traffic data from cities like NYC, London, and Stockholm, then using causal inference to measure how policies shift pollution and travel. Work spans data engineering, panel databases, statistics, and communicating results to a policy audience.

Enrollment cap: 5 students Data → Integration → Report
SYSEN 5900-663 Class nbr 19054 🏘️ Soc. Infra

Social Infrastructure & Emergency Mobility

GISUrban ResilienceEmergency Management

Does where your neighbors gather predict how well your city survives a disaster?

Cities with denser social infrastructure — parks, libraries, places of worship — tend to recover faster from disasters, but how much and where? You'll build spatial pipelines that score civic spaces across dozens of US cities, then link those scores to evacuation behavior and hazard exposure using mobility data. Work spans GIS and spatial statistics, a dashboard and API to share results, and a report connecting community infrastructure to emergency preparedness.

Enrollment cap: 5 students Data pipeline → Scoring → Report
SYSEN 5900-664 Class nbr 19055 🚲 Bikeshare

Bikeshare Network Analytics

NetworksBig DataMobility

What patterns emerge when we compare how riders move across every major US bikeshare system?

Every major US city runs a bikeshare system — Bluebikes, Citi Bike, Divvy, Capital Bikeshare — each logging millions of trips. You'll fetch and unify those records into one analysis-ready database with a shared schema, then model the resulting mobility networks to study ridership, equity, and rider behavior no single city's data can reveal alone. Work spans network science, large-scale data fetching, and database design.

Enrollment cap: 5 students Fetch → Unify → Model
SYSEN 5900-665 Class nbr 19056 🌳 Fault Tree

Agentic Fault Tree Analysis with FaultDB

ReliabilitySafetyAgentic AI

What is the probability that this system fails — and which components matter most?

Reliability engineers spend too long fighting clunky desktop tools to answer one question: how likely is this system to fail, and which parts matter most? You'll extend FaultDB — a browser platform pairing an R analysis engine with a React diagram editor — and add agentic AI workflows that help users build fault and attack trees, run cut-set analysis, and interpret the results. Work spans LLM agent design, reliability modeling, interactive front-ends, and the API behind them.

Enrollment cap: 5 students PoC → Alpha → Beta
SYSEN 5900-666 Class nbr 19057 ☢️ SAFECAST

Open-Source Radiation Monitoring with SAFECAST

SensorsOpen DataCrisis Response

After a nuclear disaster, can an open citizen-run sensor network tell people what's safe — clearly, and in real time?

SAFECAST is a Tokyo-based nonprofit that, after the 2011 Fukushima disaster, built the largest open dataset of background-radiation measurements — crowdsourced by citizens using handheld and fixed sensors, and released freely to the public. Today their network maps Fukushima, Ukraine, and cities worldwide to support crisis tracking and recovery in radiation-affected areas. In this new collaboration, your team will help SAFECAST build out the dashboard systems and AI integration behind their global sensor network so it can communicate accurate, impartial radiation information to the public when it matters most. We're recruiting students with skills in any of these areas: databases, working with sensors, APIs, React, dashboards, and data visualization — and you'll ship a working tool plus a report.

Enrollment cap: 5 students Sensors → Dashboard → AI

We are seeking…

We are seeking talented coders to leverage your data wrangling, data visualization, and data communication skills to build working tools — whatever fits the problem: React, Python, R, SQL, cloud. Prior experience with our specific stack is not required; we will train students in APIs, dashboards, and GitHub, and students will work collaboratively to build the product together. Our team contains a mix of computer science, systems, and other engineering students.

Prior R experience is required (e.g. SYSEN 5300) or significant prior web-development experience (e.g. JavaScript, HTML, CSS). All students should be ready to learn new skills. New to coding? Come ready to self-learn — and co-enroll in SYSEN 5300 (Fall · R foundation) or SYSEN 5381 (Spring) to skill up.

Distance-learning and on-campus students are both welcome.

Grading criteria

Deliverables (subgroup) — 60% Weekly progress-check attendance — 10% Weekly progress-check scores — 10% Poster presentation — 10% Final report — 10%

60% — Deliverables (subgroup)

Students will be divided up into small subgroups of their choosing, with instructor input, to tackle a series of challenges together that will help our project. Each subgroup of students will be responsible for producing 3 deliverables over the course of the term. Each deliverable should be accompanied by a co-written summary describing the deliverable, of at least 1 page, single spaced (which can be bundled into a future paper!). Each deliverable will be worth 20% of that student-team's grade. Students will decide their next deliverable together with the project advisor, a month prior to each deadline.

  • 20% — Deliverable 1: Due after month 1, with summary.
  • 20% — Deliverable 2: Due after month 2, with summary.
  • 20% — Deliverable 3: Due after month 3, with summary.

10% — Attending weekly progress checks (individual)

Signing up for and attending at least 1 progress check meeting per week with project staff. (It is strongly recommended that you sign up for a progress check at the same time as your partner each week.)

10% — Weekly progress check scores (subgroup)

At each check-in meeting, students will update the project advisor on their progress. For each check-in, they can receive one of two possible scores: progress (100%) or no progress (0%). Each term, a student gets 2 'freebies' (where you can have made no progress in a week), with no grade impact. After dropping 2 'no progress scores', your weekly progress check scores will be averaged to produce your overall grade for this component.

10% — Poster presentation (group)

At the end of term, your team will develop a public facing poster or video (details TBA) to present the product you have produced this term.

10% — Final report (group)

At the end of term, your team will need to submit to your program a report summarizing your contributions in this M.Eng. project.

Coding and replication

  • Participants are expected to code their work in fully reproducible scripts — whatever fits the problem: React, Python, R, SQL, cloud.
  • The instructor strongly encourages the use of RStudio where relevant.
  • For each deliverable, participants are required to commit and push all code and data to a repository on our team GitHub Page. Link: github.coecis.cornell.edu/CAT
